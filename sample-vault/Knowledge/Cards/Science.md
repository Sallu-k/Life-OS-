## Entropy
In an isolated system, entropy (roughly, the number of ways its parts can be arranged) never decreases. That is why heat flows from hot to cold and not back by itself.
Source: Wikipedia, Second law of thermodynamics
Q: In an isolated system, what can never decrease?
A: Entropy

## Speed of light
Light travels at exactly 299,792,458 metres per second in a vacuum; the metre itself is defined from this number.
Source: Wikipedia, Speed of light
Q: Which unit is defined using the speed of light?
A: The metre

## Light from the Sun
Sunlight takes about 8 minutes 20 seconds to reach Earth, so you always see the Sun as it was a little over eight minutes ago.
Source: Wikipedia, Sunlight
Q: About how old is the sunlight that reaches you?
A: About 8 minutes 20 seconds

## Absolute zero
Absolute zero, 0 kelvin or −273.15 °C, is the lowest possible temperature: the point where a system has the least possible energy.
Source: Wikipedia, Absolute zero
Q: What is absolute zero in degrees Celsius?
A: −273.15 °C
